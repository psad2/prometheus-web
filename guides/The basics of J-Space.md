# The Basics of Wormholes

Welcome to wormhole space.

Living here is different from living in known space. There are no gates connecting us to a fixed neighborhood, local chat does not automatically tell you who is in system, and our connections can change throughout the day.

That sounds complicated at first. It really isn't.

The most important rule is

 If you don't know what is around you, assume you don't know if you're safe.

This guide covers the basics of living in our home system. Detailed scanning and probing procedures are covered separately.

---

## 1. Our Home A C2

We live in a Class 2 (C2) wormhole system.

C2 systems are particularly useful because they have two static wormholes. Our system has

 C3 static — leads to a Class 3 wormhole system
 High-Sec static — leads to known space

When one of these statics collapses, a new wormhole of the same type will eventually be generated.

This means our home system doesn't have a permanently fixed neighborhood.

### What static means

A static does not mean that the actual wormhole stays in the same place forever.

It means that the system is guaranteed to have a connection of that particular type.

For example

```text
OUR HOME
   │
   ├── C3 STATIC ──► C3 system
   │
   └── HS STATIC ──► High-Sec system
```

If we collapse the C3 static

```text
OUR HOME
   │
   └── C3 STATIC ──► NEW C3 system
```

The destination changes, but it is still a C3 static.

The same applies to our High-Sec static.

---

# 2. Wormhole Space Has No Local

This is one of the most important things to understand.

In normal known space, seeing someone enter local gives you an immediate warning.

Wormhole space doesn't work like that.

Someone can be sitting in your system without appearing in local as a warning.

That means

 D-scan is your local chat.

If you're mining, ratting, hacking, hauling, moving between bookmarks, or simply sitting somewhere in space, you should be comfortable using D-scan.

---

# 3. D-Scan Your Basic Early-Warning System

You do not need to be an expert scanner to use D-scan.

D-scan tells you about objects around your ship, including things such as

 Ships
 Combat probes
 Core probes
 Mobile structures
 Drones
 Other objects that may indicate activity

It does not show everything in space. For example, cosmic signatures themselves aren't displayed on D-scan.

The important thing is to make D-scan part of your normal gameplay.

### When should I D-scan

At minimum

 Before starting an activity
 While doing PvE
 While mining
 After landing somewhere
 When entering a new system
 When someone reports activity
 Whenever something feels suspicious
 Periodically while sitting exposed in space (Over the thumb rule Once every ~30 Seconds)

Don't just press D-scan once and assume you're safe for the next 30 minutes.

A wormhole system can change very quickly.

### What are you looking for

The most important warning signs for a new player are

```text
Combat probes
Unknown ships 
Interdictors  heavy tackle
Stealth bombers
Scanning ships
Large numbers of ships
Unexpected drones
```

If you suddenly see probes on D-scan

 Stop what you're doing and investigate. (Ask on Comms)

Do not assume the probes belong to us.

# 4. Wormholes Are Temporary

Every wormhole has two important limitations

1. Lifetime
2. Mass

### Lifetime

A wormhole eventually disappears naturally.

The Show Info window will tell you approximately how much life remains.

Never assume a wormhole will still exist when you return through it.

---

# 5. Wormhole Mass

Wormholes also have a maximum amount of mass that can pass through them.

Every ship that jumps through removes some of the wormhole's remaining mass.

Eventually, enough ships will have passed through and the wormhole will collapse.

This is how we can intentionally roll a wormhole.

### Important

The wormhole's mass is not an exact number you can safely assume from the beginning.

Wormhole mass values can vary, so rolling requires proper procedures and communication.

Do not experiment with wormhole mass on your own.

If somebody says

 We're rolling the C3.

Do not jump through it with your battleship because you want to see what happens.

Ask the person running the roll.

---

# 6. What Does Rolling a Hole Mean

Rolling a wormhole means intentionally causing it to collapse by sending enough mass through it.

This is normally done because we want to replace the current connection with a new one.

For example, our C3 static might lead to

```text
Our C2
   │
   └── C3 static
          │
          └── C3 with nothing interesting
```

We can roll the static.

After it collapses, a new C3 static appears

```text
Our C2
   │
   └── NEW C3 static
          │
          └── potentially much more interesting chain
```

This is one of the major advantages of wormhole space

 We can effectively change our neighborhood.

---

# 7. Can a Wormhole Be Sealed

Yes — but understand what we mean by that.

A wormhole connection can be closedcollapsed by

 Its natural lifetime expiring
 Its available mass being used
 Deliberately rolling it

We can also make our immediate home system considerably harder to access by controlling and rolling its connections.

However

 Our C3 and High-Sec statics can never be permanently removed.

They will eventually be replaced by another static of the same type.

So when somebody says

 Home is sealed.

They generally mean that the currently known connections have been closedrolled and that the new connections did get scanned down but not warped to. If we dont warp to fresh connections they will not appear in Space for ~4 hours. After that they will spawn.

# 8. The New Static Problem

When a static collapses, don't assume that everything is immediately done.

A new static connection is generated.

That means the process is

```text
OLD STATIC
    ↓
COLLAPSE
    ↓
NEW SIGNATURE
    ↓
NEW STATIC WORMHOLE
    ↓
SCAN  IDENTIFY  MAP
```

The new connection must still be found and dealt with.

This is why maintaining the mapper is so important.

# 10. Why the Mapper Matters

Imagine three people are online.

Pilot A finds a wormhole.

Pilot B later enters the same system.

Pilot C is trying to get home.

Without a mapper, all three might have completely different ideas about what the chain looks like.

With a maintained mapper

```text
             C3
              │
              │
          [HOME C2]
              │
              │
             HS
```

Everyone has the same information.

The mapper is therefore not just a navigation tool.

It is shared intelligence.

Another addition of the Mapper is, that you will not get rolled out as easily. The Mapper shows who is in which system, so nobody closes the way home for someone.

# 11. If You Find a New Wormhole

If you're scanning and find a new wormhole

1. Identify what type of space it leads to.
2. Check its lifetime.
3. Check its mass state.
4. Bookmark it correctly.
5. Addupdate it on the mapper.
6. Follow our scanning procedures if you are continuing down the chain.

If you are unsure about any of this

 Ask someone before jumping.

Especially if the wormhole is

 EOL
 Mass-critical
 Being rolled
 Part of an active operation
 A connection that nobody else appears to know about

# 12. Wormhole States You Need to Know

You will hear these terms constantly.

### Fresh

A relatively newhealthy connection.

Usually means it has plenty of life and mass remaining.

### EOL

End of Life.

The wormhole is close to naturally collapsing.

Treat EOL connections carefully.

If you go through one, there is always a possibility that it will disappear before you can return.

### Shrinking  Destab  Reduced

The wormhole has lost a significant amount of its available mass.

It is still usable, but the remaining mass needs to be taken seriously.

### Critical  Crit

The wormhole is very close to collapsing from mass usage.

Do not jump through a critical hole unless you know exactly what you're doing.

A small ship is not automatically safe if the hole is already critically unstable.

### CritCrit

The wormhole has basically no mass left, because we rolled it Critical with Battleships, and it didnt close after 1 or more passes with a rolling Hic.

Do not touch these Holes AT ALL

# 13. Bookmark Wormholes Properly

When bookmarking a wormhole, bookmark the actual wormhole on grid, not simply the scanner result.

The bookmark should allow another pilot to warp directly to the wormhole.

Use the alliance's established bookmark naming convention whenever applicable.

If you're unsure how a bookmark should be named

 Ask before creating a mess on the map.

# 14. Our Ship Naming Convention

All alliance ships should use the following prefix

```text
♤
```

For example

```text
♤ My Venture
♤ C3 Raven
♤ Rolling Megathron
♤ Scout Heron
```

### Why do we do this

The prefix makes our ships immediately recognizable to our own pilots.

This is particularly useful in wormhole space because D-scan is one of our primary sources of information.

If several ships appear on D-scan, recognizable naming makes it easier to distinguish

```text
♤ Scout Heron
♤ Ratting Gila
♤ Rolling Megathron

Unknown Heron
Unknown Stratios
Unknown Proteus
```

The `♤` prefix effectively tells our pilots

 This ship is ours.

It also makes fleet coordination and D-scan checks much easier.

### Important

The prefix is not a replacement for identification.

If you see

```text
♤ Something
```

and you don't recognize the pilot, don't automatically assume they're friendly.

Names can be changed.

Always consider

 Who is flying it
 Where are they
 What are they doing
 Are they currently part of an operation
 Does the mapperfleet information agree

# 15. Don't Assume a Ship Is Friendly

This is a general wormhole rule.

Seeing a familiar ship name does not prove that the pilot is friendly.

Likewise, seeing an unfamiliar ship does not necessarily mean that someone is attacking us.

Use the available information

 D-scan
 Mapper
 Fleet chat
 Alliancecorp communication
 Character identification
 Location
 Current operations

When in doubt

 Call it out.

It is much better to say

 Unknown Proteus on D-scan in home.

than to silently assume it's nothing.

# 16. Communication Is Part of Survival

Wormhole operations depend heavily on communication.
Therefore try being on Discord at all times of playing.

If you see something unusual, report it.

Good

 `Unknown Stratios in home, near planet 4.`

Better

 `Unknown Stratios in home, combat probes visible, appears to be scanning.`

Bad

 `uh guys`

Give people useful information.

If you're being chased, say where you are and what is happening.

# 17. Don't Create Unnecessary Signatures

Be aware that activities in wormhole space can create additional signatures or otherwise change the information available to the group.

More signatures means more work for everyone maintaining situational awareness.

This is another reason to follow the alliance's procedures instead of improvising.

# 18. Before You Leave Home

Before taking an expensive ship out of the hole, check

 Is the route known
 Is the relevant static mapped
 Is the wormhole EOL
 Is its mass critical
 Are there hostileunknown pilots in the chain
 Is the mapper current
 Do you have a way back

Especially remember the last point.

In known space, you can usually rely on gates.

In wormhole space

 Your way home is another temporary connection.

# 20. If You See Combat Probes

This is one of the most important newbie reactions.

If you suddenly see combat probes

Stop.

Don't continue ratting while trying to figure out who launched them.

Immediately consider

```text
Can I safely warp out
Where is my safe
Where is the nearest known friendly
What ships are on D-scan
Which connections are open
```

Then report it.

Combat probes don't automatically mean you're about to die, but they mean someone is actively probing space.

Treat that information seriously.

---

# 21. If You Get Lost

Don't panic.

This happens to everyone in wormhole space.

Do not randomly jump through wormholes hoping you'll eventually find High-Sec.

Instead

1. Stop.
2. Open the mapper.
3. Determine where you are.
4. Identify the route back toward home.
5. Ask in alliance/corp chat if you're unsure.
6. Follow the mapped chain.

If the mapper doesn't contain your location, tell someone.

Someone may be able to help reconstruct the chain.

# 22. The Golden Rules

If you remember nothing else, remember these

### 1. D-scan is your local.

Use it constantly.

### 2. Keep the mapper open.

Know what is connected to home.

### 3. Update the mapper.

If you find it, map it.

If it disappears, update it.

### 4. Never casually jump a criticalEOL hole.

You may not get back.

### 5. Don't roll holes without asking.

A wormhole's mass is a shared resource.

### 6. Don't assume you're safe because nobody is in local.

There is no local safety net in wormhole space.

### 7. Report unknown activity.

Better to over-report something harmless than miss a hostile scout.

### 8. Use the `♤` prefix on alliance ships.

It helps everyone identify friendly ships on D-scan.

### 9. Don't rely exclusively on ship names.

Verify who and what you're looking at.

### 10. When in doubt, ask.

Nobody expects a new pilot to know everything immediately.

# The Wormhole Mindset

The biggest change when moving into wormhole space is your mindset.

Don't think

 Is this system safe

Think

 What information do I have about this system, and how recently did I check it

Wormhole space is constantly changing.

Connections appear and disappear.
People enter and leave.
Statics get rolled.
New chains form.
Unknown pilots can be sitting silently in system.

Your job as a wormhole pilot isn't to know that nothing is happening.

Your job is to notice when something changes.

Stay on the mapper.
Use D-scan.
Communicate.
And don't be afraid to ask questions.
